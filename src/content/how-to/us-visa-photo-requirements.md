# US Visa Photo Requirements (2x2 Inch Size and Rules)

> **Quick answer:** A US visa photo is **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)** with your head **1 to 1⅜ inches (25 to 35 mm)** tall. For the DS-160 upload, the photo must be **square**, between **600 × 600 and 1200 × 1200 pixels**, a **JPEG** file of **240 kB or less**. Use a **plain white or off-white background**, and the photo must have been **taken in the last 6 months**.

## US visa photo size and format

### Printed photo size

A printed US visa photo must be **2 inches wide × 2 inches high**, which is **51 × 51 mm**. It is square, not the tall 35 × 45 mm shape used in the UK.

Your head, measured from the bottom of your chin to the top of your head, must be **between 1 inch and 1⅜ inches (25 mm to 35 mm)** tall. The photo must be in colour and printed on photo-quality paper.

### Digital photo size and file type for DS-160

Most visa applicants upload a digital photo with the online DS-160 form. The State Department's digital image rules are:

- **Shape:** square (the width and height must be the same)
- **Minimum:** 600 × 600 pixels
- **Maximum:** 1200 × 1200 pixels
- **File type:** JPEG (.jpg)
- **File size:** 240 kB or less
- **Compression:** a ratio of 20:1 or less

The State Department's own photo tool crops images to exactly 600 × 600 pixels, which is a safe size to aim for.

## Background, lighting and pose

### White background rule

Your background must be **plain white or off-white**, with no pattern, texture or objects. UK printed photos use cream or light grey instead.

There should be **no shadows** on the background or on your face. Even light from in front of you is the easiest way to avoid them.

### Neutral expression and facing the camera

Face the camera directly with your full face in view. Keep your head straight, not tilted, and both eyes open.

Use a natural, neutral expression. The photo must not be digitally enhanced or altered in any way to change how you look, so switch off beauty modes and filters.

### Glasses, head coverings and earphones

**Glasses are not allowed** in new US visa photos. The only exception is a rare medical case where you cannot remove them, and then you need a signed medical statement.

**Hats and head coverings are not allowed**, unless you wear one for religious reasons or for a medical reason. For religious attire, you need a signed statement that it is traditional religious attire worn continuously in public. Your full face must be visible, and the covering must not cast shadows on your face.

The rules we could confirm do not list earphones by name. To be safe, remove headphones, earphones and anything else that covers your head, ears or face.

## How to take a US visa photo at home

### Step-by-step

1. **Find a plain white or off-white wall.** Stand in front of a plain white or off-white wall with no pattern, texture or objects behind you. Stand about half a metre away from it so you don't cast a shadow.
2. **Face a window for even light.** Use daylight from a window in front of you. Turn off the flash and avoid overhead lights that cast shadows under your eyes and nose.
3. **Remove glasses and anything on your head.** Take off your glasses, hat and anything else covering your head or face, unless you wear a head covering for religious or medical reasons.
4. **Ask someone to take the photo.** Ask a helper to hold the phone at your eye level, about one to one and a half metres away, and use the rear camera.
5. **Pose with a neutral expression.** Face the camera straight on, with your head upright, both eyes open and a natural, neutral expression.
6. **Take several shots and crop to a square.** Take five or six photos, pick the sharpest, then crop it to a square with your head between 50% and 69% of the image height.

### Distance, height and light

Hold the camera about **1 to 1.5 metres** from your face. Any closer and a phone lens can distort your features.

Keep the camera at the **same height as your eyes**, not above or below. Soft daylight from a window gives the most even light. Avoid direct sunlight, which causes harsh shadows and squinting.

### Mistakes to avoid

- Using the UK 35 × 45 mm size instead of a square photo
- A cream, grey or coloured background instead of white or off-white
- Wearing glasses, even if they have no glare
- Your head too small or too large in the square
- Shadows behind your head from standing too close to the wall
- Beauty filters, smoothing or a replaced background
- Using a photo older than 6 months

### What the official photo tool checks

The State Department provides its own online photo tool. You can choose a digital image from your computer, resize and rotate it if needed, and crop it to a square of exactly **600 × 600 pixels**.

It helps with size and cropping only, so lighting, background and pose still need to be right.

### What to do if the DS-160 upload fails

Check that the file is a square JPEG of at least 600 × 600 pixels and **240 kB or less**. Phone photos are often too large, so export a smaller copy.

If the system still rejects your photo, you can usually carry on with the form. In that case, **bring one printed 2 × 2 inch photo to your visa interview**. The final decision on your photo is always made by the US embassy or consulate.

## US visa photo vs UK passport photo

### Size difference (2x2 inch vs 35x45 mm)

| | US visa | UK passport (printed) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Size | 2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm) | 35 × 45 mm |
| Shape | Square | Portrait (7:9) |
| Head height | 25 to 35 mm | 29 to 34 mm |
| Background | White or off-white | Cream or light grey |
| How recent | Last 6 months | Last month |

A US visa photo is wider and square. A UK passport photo is narrower and taller. For more on the UK shape, see our guide to [35x45mm in pixels, inches and cm](/how-to/35x45mm-in-pixels-inches-cm).

### Background and head-size differences

The US asks for **white or off-white**. The UK asks for **cream or light grey** for printed photos, and a plain light-coloured background for digital ones.

Glasses are another key difference. The UK says not to wear glasses unless you have to, and then only with no glare or reflection. The US does not allow them at all, except in rare medical cases.

### Can I use a UK passport photo for a US visa?

No. A UK passport photo is the wrong shape and size, and its background may be too grey or cream. Stretching it into a square distorts your face.

Instead, crop your original photo again to a square. If it was taken against a white or off-white wall in the last 6 months, it may work for both. For the full UK rules, read our [UK passport photo requirements](/how-to/uk-passport-photo-requirements) guide.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size is a US visa photo?

A printed US visa photo is 2 × 2 inches, or 51 × 51 mm. Your head must be 1 to 1⅜ inches tall from chin to top. For the DS-160 upload, the digital photo must be square, between 600 × 600 and 1200 × 1200 pixels, and a JPEG of 240 kB or less.

### Can I take a US visa photo with my phone?

Yes. A modern phone camera is good enough. Ask someone to take it at eye level from about 1 to 1.5 metres, against a plain white or off-white wall in daylight. Then crop it to a square and check that the JPEG file is 240 kB or less before you upload.

### Can I smile in a US visa photo?

Keep a natural, neutral expression with both eyes open and your face fully visible. A wide smile changes the shape of your face, which can make the photo harder to match to you. A relaxed, neutral face gives your photo a good chance of passing.

### How old can my US visa photo be?

Your photo must have been taken within the last 6 months. It should show how you look now, so take a new one if your appearance has changed, for example after growing or removing a beard. Do not reuse a photo from a previous visa or passport.

### Can I wear glasses?

No. Glasses are not allowed in new US visa photos. The only exception is a rare medical case where you cannot remove them, and you then need a signed medical statement. In the UK, you can wear them if you have to, as long as there is no glare.
