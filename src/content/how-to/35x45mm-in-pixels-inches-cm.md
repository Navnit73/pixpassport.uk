# 35x45mm Photo Size in Pixels, Inches and CM

> **Quick answer:** A 35x45mm photo is **35 mm wide × 45 mm high**. That is **3.5 × 4.5 cm** or **1.38 × 1.77 inches**. At **300 DPI** (standard for printing) it is **413 × 531 pixels**. At **600 DPI** it is **827 × 1063 pixels**. The shape, or aspect ratio, is **7:9**.

## 35x45mm photo size in cm and inches

### Millimetres to centimetres

There are 10 millimetres in a centimetre, so divide each side by 10. A 35x45mm photo is exactly **3.5 cm wide × 4.5 cm high**.

### Millimetres to inches

There are 25.4 millimetres in an inch. Divide each side by 25.4:

- 35 mm ÷ 25.4 = **1.38 inches** wide
- 45 mm ÷ 25.4 = **1.77 inches** high

### Quick conversion table

| Unit        | Width   | Height  |
| :---------- | :------ | :------ |
| Millimetres | 35 mm   | 45 mm   |
| Centimetres | 3.5 cm  | 4.5 cm  |
| Inches      | 1.38 in | 1.77 in |

## 35x45mm in pixels at different DPI

### The pixel formula

Pixels depend on DPI (dots per inch). The formula is simple:

**pixels = (millimetres ÷ 25.4) × DPI**

For example, at 300 DPI the width is (35 ÷ 25.4) × 300 = **413 pixels** and the height is (45 ÷ 25.4) × 300 = **531 pixels**.

### 35x45mm pixel size table

| DPI     | Width × height | Best for                                    |
| :------ | :------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| 100 DPI | 138 × 177 px   | Screen preview only                         |
| 150 DPI | 207 × 266 px   | Draft prints                                |
| 300 DPI | 413 × 531 px   | Standard photo printing                     |
| 600 DPI | 827 × 1063 px  | High-quality printing from a sharp original |

If you have seen other numbers online, such as 414 × 571 px, check the shape. Those figures describe a taller photo, not 35 × 45 mm.

### Head size in pixels (UK passport print)

GOV.UK says the image of you, from the crown of your head to your chin, must be between **29 mm and 34 mm** high on a printed UK passport photo. In pixels that is about:

- **343 to 402 px high** at 300 DPI
- **685 to 803 px high** at 600 DPI

### Does DPI change quality?

No. DPI does not add pixels. It only changes how large the photo prints. Quality comes from the number of pixels in your original photo, so a small, blurry image will not get sharper at 600 DPI.

## Which size should you use: print or online upload?

### For printing

Use **413 × 531 px at 300 DPI** for most prints. Use 600 DPI only if your original photo is large and sharp. Set the print to 100% (actual size), because "fit to page" changes the size.

### For online upload forms

Online forms set rules in pixels, not millimetres. Always follow the form. For a UK online passport application, GOV.UK asks for a digital photo that is **at least 600 pixels wide and 750 pixels tall**, between **50KB and 10MB**.

That means a 413 × 531 px print file is **too small** for the UK online minimum. If you take the photo on your own device during the application, GOV.UK says to include your head, shoulders and upper body and **not to crop it**, because the service crops it for you.

### Printed vs digital UK passport photos

|            | Printed (paper form)      | Digital (online)      |
| :--------- | :------------------------ | :-------------------- |
| Size       | 45 mm high × 35 mm wide   | At least 600 × 750 px |
| File size  | Not applicable            | 50KB to 10MB          |
| Background | Plain cream or light grey | Plain light-coloured  |
| Number     | 2 identical prints        | 1 photo               |

## What is the 35x45 aspect ratio?

### The 7:9 ratio explained

The aspect ratio is the shape of the photo. Divide 35 and 45 by 5 and you get **7:9**: for every 7 units of width, the photo is 9 units tall. Any 7:9 image, such as 827 × 1063 px, prints at exactly 35 × 45 mm without stretching.

### 35x45 vs 600 × 750 pixels (4:5)

The UK online minimum of 600 × 750 pixels is a **4:5 ratio**, not 7:9. The shapes are close but not the same. This is why you should not squeeze a print-size file into an online upload: let the online service do the cropping.

### Why a wrong ratio causes problems

If you resize a photo to 35 × 45 mm without keeping the 7:9 ratio, your face is stretched or squashed. Crop to the right shape first, then resize.

## Where is a 35x45mm photo used?

### UK passport

A printed UK passport photo must measure 45 mm high by 35 mm wide, the standard size used in UK photo booths. GOV.UK also says it must not be a cut-down version of a larger picture. For all the other rules, see our [UK passport photo requirements](/how-to/uk-passport-photo-requirements).

### UK driving licence

The DVLA asks for a "recent printed passport type photo" on postal applications, so the same 35 × 45 mm print is the safe choice. See our [driving licence photo requirements](/how-to/driving-licence-photo-requirements-uk).

### Schengen visas and European documents

Many European passports, ID cards and Schengen visa applications also use 35 × 45 mm. Head size and background can differ by country, so always check your own checklist. Our [Schengen visa photo tool](/tool/schengen-visa-photo) can crop to this size.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 35x45mm the same as 3.5x4.5 cm?

Yes. 35x45mm and 3.5x4.5 cm are exactly the same size in different units. There are 10 millimetres in a centimetre, so 35 mm is 3.5 cm and 45 mm is 4.5 cm. Shops, forms and apps may use either unit, so treat them as identical.

### What is 35x45mm in pixels?

At 300 DPI, 35x45mm is 413 × 531 pixels. At 600 DPI it is 827 × 1063 pixels. At 100 DPI it is 138 × 177 pixels. Use the formula pixels = (mm ÷ 25.4) × DPI for any other resolution. Always follow your form if it gives its own pixel rules.

### What is 35x45mm in inches?

35x45mm is about 1.38 inches wide by 1.77 inches high. You get this by dividing each side by 25.4, the number of millimetres in an inch. Fractions such as 1⅜ × 1¾ inches are close but slightly smaller, so use millimetres when you can.

### Is 35x45mm the same as passport size?

For a UK passport, yes: a printed photo is 35 mm wide and 45 mm high. Other countries use different "passport sizes", such as the 2 × 2 inch photo for US visas. Always check the size your document asks for.

### Is 35x45 the same as 45x35?

They describe the same photo, but the order matters. Sizes are usually written width first, so 35x45 means 35 mm wide and 45 mm high, a portrait photo. GOV.UK writes it as 45 mm high by 35 mm wide. A photo that is 45 mm wide would be the wrong way round.

### What DPI do I need for a 35x45mm photo?

Use 300 DPI for most prints. That gives 413 × 531 pixels and a sharp result on photo paper. Choose 600 DPI only if your original photo is large and sharp. DPI does not matter for online uploads, where the pixel size and file size rules on the form apply.

### Can I use a 35x45mm photo for a US visa?

No. US visa photos are 2 × 2 inches (square), so a 7:9 photo does not match. Use a tool made for US visas, such as our [US visa photo tool](/tool/us-visa-photo-tool).
