## Can You Take Your Own Passport Photo at Home?

Yes. HM Passport Office accepts digital photos that you take yourself, so you don't need a photo booth or a studio. All you need is a smartphone, a plain wall and some daylight.

The catch is that your photo must meet strict rules. GOV.UK asks for a **clear, in-focus, recent colour photo** with a plain light-coloured background, no shadows and a neutral expression. Photos that break these rules are one of the most common reasons for a delayed application. Taking a passport picture at home is easy once you know what to check, and the steps below cover it.

## What You Need to Take a Passport Photo at Home

- A smartphone with a decent camera (any recent phone will do)
- A plain, light-coloured wall or a plain sheet or board to use as a backdrop
- A window with soft daylight
- A tripod, a stack of books or a helper to hold the phone
- About 15 minutes

## How to Take a Passport Photo at Home in 7 Steps

1. **Choose a plain background.** Stand in front of a plain, light-coloured wall with no patterns, texture or objects behind you. Cream or light grey works best.
2. **Use soft, even daylight.** Face a window during the day so light falls evenly on your face. Turn off the flash and avoid direct sun or overhead lights.
3. **Set up your phone.** Prop your phone at eye level on a tripod or stack of books, or ask someone to take the photo. Keep the camera about one to one and a half metres away.
4. **Get your pose right.** Face the camera straight on with your head upright. Keep a neutral expression, your mouth closed and both eyes open and clearly visible.
5. **Remove glasses and head coverings.** Take off glasses, and move hair away from your eyes. Remove hats and head coverings unless you wear one for religious or medical reasons.
6. **Take several photos.** Take at least five or six shots so you have a choice. Use the rear camera if you can, and don't use filters or beauty modes.
7. **Check, crop and upload.** Pick the sharpest photo, crop it to head and shoulders, and run it through a photo checker before you upload it with your passport application.

## UK Passport Photo Requirements at a Glance

| Requirement    | What GOV.UK expects                                                 |
| :------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Background     | Plain, light-coloured (cream or light grey), no pattern or texture  |
| Lighting       | Even, with no shadows on your face or behind you                    |
| Expression     | Neutral, mouth closed, eyes open                                    |
| Pose           | Facing forward, looking straight at the camera                      |
| Glasses        | Not allowed, unless you can't remove them for medical reasons       |
| Head coverings | Only for religious or medical reasons, with your face fully visible |
| Quality        | In focus, clear and not blurred, with no red-eye, glare or filters  |
| Age of photo   | Taken within the last month                                         |
| Digital size   | At least 600 pixels wide and 750 pixels tall, as a JPG              |

These rules can change, so check the latest guidance on GOV.UK before you apply. Rules for babies and young children are different, and our [UK passport picture rules](/tool/passport-picture-rules-uk) guide covers them in full.

## How to Take a Passport Photo From Your Mobile

Most people use their phone, and a few settings make a big difference:

- **Use the rear camera.** It gives a sharper image than the front camera. Ask someone to take the photo, or use a tripod and a timer.
- **Don't take a selfie at arm's length.** Close-up selfies distort your face because the camera is too near. Stand back and let the phone sit at eye level.
- **Switch off filters, HDR and beauty mode.** These change your skin and features, which can get the photo rejected.
- **Keep the phone level.** Tilting it up or down changes how your head looks in the frame.
- **Use portrait orientation** and leave space above your head and around your shoulders so you can crop later.

If you only have a phone and no helper, a timer and a stack of books can replace a tripod.

## Common Mistakes When You Do a Passport Photo at Home

- **Shadows behind your head or on your face.** Step away from the wall and face the window. Then run the photo through our [UK passport photo checker](/tool/passport-photo-checker-uk) to spot any shadows that remain.
- **A busy or dark background.** Use a plain, light wall, not a door, curtain or patterned wallpaper.
- **Smiling or tilting your head.** Keep a neutral face and hold your head upright.
- **Photos that are too dark or blurry.** Use bright but indirect daylight, and hold the camera still.
- **Editing or filtering the photo.** Don't retouch it, smooth it or change the colours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I take my own passport photo at home?

Yes. You can take your own passport photo at home with a smartphone or camera, as long as it meets the UK photo rules. Use a plain light background, even lighting and a neutral expression.

### How do I take a passport size photo from my mobile?

Use the rear camera on a tripod or ask someone to take it, with the phone at eye level about one to one and a half metres away. Face forward against a plain wall in daylight, then crop the photo to head and shoulders. A [passport photo maker](/passport-size-photo-maker) can crop it to the right size for you.

### What background do I need for a passport photo at home?

A plain, light-coloured background, such as cream or light grey, with no patterns, textures or shadows. A white or pale wall that is evenly lit works well.

### Can I take a passport photo with a selfie?

It's better not to. Arm's-length selfies can distort your face and tilt your head. Ask someone to take it, or set your phone on a tripod or a stack of books.

### How do I take my own ID photo at home?

The same method works for most ID photos: a plain background, soft daylight, a straight-on pose and a neutral expression. Always check the specific size and background rules for the ID you are applying for, as they can differ from UK passport photos.

### Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?

No, unless you can't remove them for medical reasons. Take them off, so there is no glare or frame covering your eyes.

### How recent does my passport photo need to be?

It needs to have been taken within the last month, so don't reuse an old photo.
