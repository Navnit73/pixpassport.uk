# UK Driving Licence Photo Requirements (Can You Take Your Own?)

> **Quick answer:** Yes, you can take your own driving licence photo. The DVLA asks for a "recent printed passport type photo" for postal applications, so follow the UK passport photo rules: **45 mm high × 35 mm wide**, plain background, plain expression, no shadows. If you renew online, you can use your passport photo or upload a new one. Online renewal costs **£14** and your new licence should arrive within a week.

## Can I take my own driving licence photo?

Yes. The DVLA does not ask for a professional photographer. What matters is that the photo looks like you today and follows passport-style rules.

### Online applications

When you renew your licence online on GOV.UK, you can use your **passport photo** or **upload a new one**. A photo you take yourself at home can work, as long as it is clear, in focus and evenly lit.

You must be a resident of Great Britain and not be disqualified from driving. You cannot renew online if your name or title has changed, or if you have a 5-year bus or lorry licence.

### Paper applications

If you apply by post, you need a D1 pack from a Post Office and a **recent printed passport type photo** with your forms. GOV.UK says **not to sign the back** of the photo.

You can take this photo yourself and print it, but it must be printed at the right size on proper photographic paper. A blurry home printout on plain paper has a much lower chance of passing.

### When a photo shop or booth is safer

A booth or shop photo is a good choice if you do not have a plain wall, good light or someone to help. It is also safer for postal applications if you do not have a photo printer.

If you would rather save money, a phone photo checked with our [UK driving licence photo tool](/tool/uk-driving-licence-photo) works well too.

## What size and format is a driving licence photo?

The DVLA pages do not list a size in millimetres. They ask for a "passport type photo" and link to the GOV.UK passport photo rules. So the safest choice is to follow the UK passport photo size.

### Printed photo size

A UK passport-style print is **45 mm high × 35 mm wide**, the standard size used in UK photo booths. Your head, from the crown to your chin, should measure between **29 mm and 34 mm**. It should be a close-up of your full head and upper shoulders.

The photo must be in colour on plain white photographic paper with no border, clear, in focus, and with no creases or tears.

### Digital photo size and file type

The DVLA does not publish separate pixel or file size rules for licence photos. The GOV.UK passport rules ask for digital photos that are **at least 600 pixels wide and 750 pixels tall**, between **50KB and 10MB**. Use that as your guide, and follow any on-screen instructions in the licence service.

A standard phone photo is far bigger than the minimum, so resolution is rarely the problem.

## What are the background, lighting and pose rules?

The DVLA links to the passport photo rules, so use those as your checklist. Following them closely gives your photo the best chance of passing.

### Plain light background

Use a plain background with no pattern, objects or other people. For printed photos, GOV.UK says cream or light grey. For digital photos, it says plain and light-coloured, in clear contrast to you. Stand away from the wall so you do not cast a shadow behind your head.

### Face position and neutral expression

Face forwards and look straight at the camera. Keep a plain expression with your mouth closed and your eyes open and visible. There must be no hair in front of your eyes, no red eye, and no shadows on your face or behind you.

### Glasses, hats and head coverings

Do not wear glasses unless you have to. If you must, they cannot be sunglasses or tinted, and the frames, glare, reflection or shadow must not cover your eyes.

Head coverings are only allowed for religious or medical reasons, and your face must stay fully visible.

## How to take a driving licence photo at home

All you need is a phone, a plain wall, daylight and ideally a friend to help. It takes about 15 minutes. The steps below are practical advice that follows the passport rules.

### Step-by-step with a phone

1. **Find a plain, light wall.** Stand about half a metre in front of a plain cream, light grey or off-white wall with nothing on it.
2. **Face a window.** Use soft daylight from a window in front of you. Turn off the flash and overhead lights so there are no shadows.
3. **Set the phone at eye level.** Ask someone to hold the phone, or rest it on a shelf, about one to one and a half metres away and level with your eyes.
4. **Pose with a plain expression.** Look straight at the camera with your mouth closed and your eyes open. Keep your hair away from your eyes.
5. **Take several shots.** Use the rear camera with filters and beauty mode switched off. Take five or six photos and pick the sharpest one.
6. **Crop and check.** Crop the photo to show your full head and the top of your shoulders, then check it before you upload or print it.

### Getting the framing right

Your head and the top of your shoulders should fill most of the frame. Leave a small gap above your head so the crown is not cut off.

Keep your head level and square to the camera. Avoid arm's-length selfies, which distort your face. Ask someone else to take the photo, as GOV.UK does for passport photos.

### Checking it before you upload

Zoom in and look for shadows, blur, glare on glasses or hair over your eyes. If anything looks off, retake the photo rather than editing it. GOV.UK says photos must be unaltered by computer software.

## How to change the photo on your driving licence

You cannot swap the photo on its own. GOV.UK says you need to **renew your full or provisional licence** to update your photo. It costs **£14 online**, **£17 by post** or **£21.50 at a Post Office**, and it is free if you are 70 or over.

## Provisional licence photo rules

A provisional licence follows the same passport-style photo standard. The points below cover what GOV.UK says about applying and renewing.

### First provisional licence

To apply for your first provisional licence you must be at least **15 years and 9 months** old and able to read a number plate from 20 metres away. It costs **£34 online** or **£43 by post** with form D1, and should arrive within one week if you apply online.

The first provisional page does not list separate photo rules, so follow the passport-style rules in this guide and any instructions shown during your application.

### Renewing or replacing a provisional licence

You can renew a provisional licence in the same way as a full licence, and renewing is how you update the photo. Photocard licences must be renewed every 10 years.

If your licence is lost, stolen, damaged or destroyed, a replacement costs **£20 online** and should arrive within a week.

### Tips for young applicants

Use a recent photo that looks like you now, not an old school picture. Ask a parent or friend to take it, because framing and lighting are much easier with help. Take the photo against a plain, light wall without a hat or hood.

## Common driving licence photo mistakes

### Why photos get rejected

- Shadows on your face or behind your head
- A patterned, dark or busy background
- Smiling, an open mouth or a tilted head
- Glare on glasses, or tinted lenses
- Blurry, low-quality or heavily edited images
- Beauty filters or an app-changed background
- A signature on the back of a printed photo

### How to fix each mistake

Shadows and glare are fixed by facing a window and switching off the flash. Background problems are fixed by moving to a plain, light wall and stepping away from it.

For pose problems, retake the photo with a plain expression and your head level. If you are unsure, read our guide on [why passport photos get rejected](/how-to/passport-photo-rejected-uk), as the same rules apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a passport photo for my driving licence?

Yes. When you renew or change your photo online, GOV.UK says you can use your passport photo or upload a new one. For postal applications, the DVLA asks for a recent printed passport-type photo. Make sure it still looks like you, and do not sign the back.

### Can I take my own driving licence photo?

Yes. You can take your own photo as long as it follows passport-style rules: plain light background, plain expression, eyes open, no shadows and no editing. Ask someone to take it so it is level and sharp, and check it before you upload or print it.

### Does my driving licence photo have to be recent?

Yes. The DVLA asks for a recent photo. It does not give a time limit on its licence pages, but the passport rules it links to ask for a photo taken in the last month. Taking a new photo within the last month is the safest choice.

### What size is a UK driving licence photo?

The DVLA does not state a size of its own. It asks for a passport-type photo, which in the UK is 45 mm high by 35 mm wide when printed. For digital uploads, the passport minimum is 600 × 750 pixels and 50KB to 10MB.

### Can I update my driving licence photo without renewing?

No. GOV.UK says you need to renew your full or provisional licence to update your photo. Online renewal costs £14 and your new licence should arrive within a week. Renewing at a Post Office costs £21.50, and by post costs £17.

### Can I wear glasses in my driving licence photo?

It is best not to. The passport rules the DVLA points to say not to wear glasses unless you have to. If you must, they cannot be sunglasses or tinted, and the frames, glare or reflections must not cover your eyes.

### Can I upload a selfie?

You can upload a photo you took yourself, but an arm's-length selfie often causes problems. It can distort your face, tilt your head and add shadows. Ask someone to take the photo, or prop your phone at eye level, about one to one and a half metres away.

### How long does a new photo take to appear on my licence?

Your new photo appears when your new licence arrives. Online renewals should arrive within a week. Post Office and postal renewals should arrive within 3 weeks, though it can take longer if your medical or personal details need checking.
