# Best Passport Photo Options: Stores, Studios & Apps Compared

> **Quick answer:** For most people applying online, the **best passport photo** is one you take at home and check with an online tool. It's cheap, quick and you can retake it as many times as you like. Choose a **photo booth** (about £10) if you want printed photos and a GOV.UK photo code in five minutes. Choose a **staffed studio** such as Timpson or Snappy Snaps (about £11–£13) for babies or if you want someone to check the photo for you. Choose the **Post Office** only if you also want staff to check your whole application.

## Best Passport Photo Options by Need

### Best for speed

**Winner: a photo booth with a photo code (about £10).** Most Photo-Me and Max Spielmann booths in supermarkets and stations take your photo, print a strip and give you a GOV.UK photo code in about five minutes. You type the code into your online application, so there is nothing to upload.

**Runner-up: an online tool on your phone.** If you already have a plain light wall, you can take, check and download a photo in under ten minutes without leaving home. Booths win if you also need prints straight away.

Check the screen before you pay. Some older booths only print photos and do not give a code.

### Best for price

**Winner: take it yourself and upload it to GOV.UK (free).** The online passport service lets you upload your own digital photo at no cost. The risk is that you only find out about problems such as shadows or the wrong crop when you upload it, or later from an examiner.

**Best paid value: an online maker with a checker (£4.90–£7.99).** For a few pounds you get a photo cropped to the rules and checked for common problems first. PhotoGov is the cheapest we found for a UK digital photo at £4.90. PixPassport.uk checks for free and charges £7.99 for the download and a printable 6×4″ sheet.

Booths (about £10) and staffed shops (about £11–£13) cost more but include prints.

### Best for babies

**Winner: at home on your phone.** Babies under 1 can be photographed lying on a plain light sheet, taken from above, which you cannot do in a booth. Babies under 1 can have their eyes closed, and children under 6 do not need a plain expression. Take as many shots as you need while your baby is calm, then size it with our [baby passport photo maker](/tool/uk-baby-passport-photo).

**Runner-up: a staffed studio.** Timpson, Snappy Snaps and Max Spielmann staff are used to photographing children and can help hold a toddler's attention. Avoid booths for anyone too small to sit still on the stool alone.

See our full [UK baby passport photo rules](/how-to/baby-passport-photo-rules-uk) before you start.

### Best digital option

**Winner: an online tool that checks the photo before you download it.** It gives you a file that meets the GOV.UK digital rules (at least 600 × 750 pixels, 50 KB to 10 MB) that you can upload straight away. A good tool also tells you *why* a photo fails, so you can fix it.

If you would rather not upload a file at all, use a booth or shop that gives you a **photo code**. GOV.UK says photos from a booth or shop are more likely to be approved than photos taken on your own device. A code saves you uploading anything.

### Best acceptance guarantee

**Winner: a staffed studio.** Most staffed shops check your photo on the spot and many will retake it free if it is rejected. Ask about the policy at the counter and keep your receipt.

**Online, guarantees vary a lot.** Read the small print before you pay:

- **PixPassport.uk:** free re-process or 100% refund if HM Passport Office rejects the photo for size, crop or sizing errors made by our tool. You need to show the rejection notice. Problems you were warned about, such as shadows in your original photo, are not covered.
- **PhotoGov:** free retake or full refund if a photo is rejected "because of an error on our side". It also sells an optional human check for $4.90.
- **IDPhoto.us:** says it gives a refund if a government agency rejects the photo.
- **Passport Photo Checker (passport-photo-checker.com):** says it "cannot guarantee approval".
- **PixPassport.com:** we found no acceptance guarantee on its homepage.

No online tool can promise HM Passport Office will accept your photo, because an examiner makes the final decision.

## Best Passport Photo Studios: What to Look For

If you would rather have someone else take your photo, these are the main high-street choices:

- **Timpson:** about £12.99 in store with prints and a digital code.
- **Snappy Snaps:** about £10.99–£12.99 with a digital code.
- **Max Spielmann:** about £12.99 in staffed shops. Its self-service booths in Tesco and Asda are cheaper.
- **Post Office:** photos are part of Digital Check & Send, which costs £16 on top of the passport fee at selected branches.
- **Boots:** many Boots stores no longer have passport photo booths. Check your local branch before you go.

Wherever you go, check these five things:

1. **They give a GOV.UK photo code** if you are applying online.
2. **The background is plain light grey or cream**, with even light and no shadows.
3. **They follow UK rules**, not another country's: 35 × 45 mm, head 29–34 mm from chin to crown.
4. **A free retake** if HM Passport Office rejects the photo. Ask before you pay and keep the receipt.
5. **They can photograph children**, if you are getting a baby or child photo.

If you are applying on paper, you need **two identical printed photos**, and someone may need to countersign one. Read our [UK passport photo size guide](/how-to/passport-photo-size-uk) for the exact measurements.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best place to get passport photos in the UK?

It depends on what you need. For the lowest cost, take your own photo and upload it to GOV.UK. For speed and prints, use a photo booth that gives a photo code. For babies or extra help, use a staffed shop such as Timpson or Snappy Snaps. Online tools are a good middle ground if you want your photo checked first.

### Are photo booth passport photos accepted?

Yes, if the booth is set up for UK passport photos. GOV.UK says photos from a booth or shop are more likely to be approved than photos taken on your own device. Choose the passport option, check that the booth gives a photo code if you are applying online, and keep a plain expression with your face fully visible.

### Can I take my own passport photo for a UK passport?

Yes. When you apply online you can upload a digital photo you took yourself. It must be at least 600 × 750 pixels, taken in the last month, against a plain light background with no shadows, filters or editing. Ask someone else to take it, as selfies are not allowed for adults.

### What is the cheapest way to get a UK passport photo?

Uploading your own photo to the online passport service is free. If you want it checked and cropped first, online tools cost about £4.90 to £8. Booths cost about £10 and staffed shops about £11 to £13.

### Do online passport photo apps give a GOV.UK photo code?

No. Only booths and shops registered with HM Passport Office give photo codes. With an online tool, you download a digital file and upload it during your online application, which GOV.UK allows.

### Which passport photo option has the best guarantee?

Staffed shops usually offer the most practical guarantee, a free retake on the spot. Online guarantees vary. Some refund you only if the tool made a sizing error, and some offer no guarantee at all. Always read the refund policy before paying.

### Is a passport photo app safe to use?

Check the privacy policy before you upload. Look for clear statements about how long photos are stored and that they are not sold or used to train other tools. Avoid apps that apply beauty filters, because edited photos break the UK rules.
