# Passport Photo Rejected in the UK? Reasons and How to Fix Them

> **Quick answer:** Most UK passport photos are rejected for five reasons. **Shadows** (face a window and stand away from the wall). **Glare or glasses** (take them off if you can). **A smile or open mouth** (keep a plain expression). **A busy or dark background** (use plain light grey or cream). **Editing or filters** (switch them all off). Fix the problem, take a new photo and check it before you apply again.

## Why was my UK passport photo rejected?

GOV.UK says your application will be delayed if your photos do not meet the rules. These are the most common problems.

### The 12 most common reasons (table: Problem, Why it fails, Fix)

| # | Problem | Why it fails | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Shadow on the face | Part of your face is hidden or darker | Face a window and turn off overhead lights |
| 2 | Shadow behind the head | The outline of your head is unclear | Stand about half a metre from the wall |
| 3 | Glare on glasses | Your eyes cannot be seen clearly | Take your glasses off unless you must wear them |
| 4 | Eyes closed or red eye | Eyes must be open, visible and natural | Turn off the flash and take several shots |
| 5 | Smiling or open mouth | A plain expression is required | Relax your face and close your mouth |
| 6 | Head tilted or turned | You must face forwards | Look straight into the lens with your chin level |
| 7 | Patterned or dark background | The background must be plain and light | Use a plain light grey or cream wall |
| 8 | Edited background | Photos must be unaltered by software | Retake against a real plain wall |
| 9 | Blurry or low resolution | Photos must be clear and in focus | Use the rear camera and hold it steady |
| 10 | Wrong size or bad crop | Head size or frame is outside the rules | Print at 35 × 45 mm with a 29–34 mm head |
| 11 | Damaged or wrong paper | Prints must be professional standard | Use plain white photo paper with no creases |
| 12 | Photo too old | It must be taken in the last month | Take a new photo just before you apply |

### Online photo checks vs human examiner checks

When you apply online, the service checks your photo as you upload it and may warn you about problems such as poor lighting. Passing that check is a good sign, but a passport examiner still looks at your photo later. Printed photos sent with a paper form are only checked by people.

### How to find the exact reason in your application

If you applied online, read any messages in your online account and emails from HM Passport Office. If you sent a paper form, keep any letter you receive, because it tells you what to send next. If anything is unclear, contact HM Passport Office using the details on GOV.UK.

## Rejection reasons about the face and pose

### Shadows on the face or behind the head

**Problem:** One side of your face is darker, or there is a dark outline around your head. **Cause:** Light from one side or above, or standing too close to the wall. **Fix:** Face a window in daylight, switch off ceiling lights and the flash, and stand about half a metre from the wall.

### Closed eyes, red eye or glare on glasses

**Problem:** Your eyes are shut, red or hidden by reflections. **Cause:** Blinking, using the flash, or light bouncing off your lenses. **Fix:** Turn off the flash, take several shots and pick one where both eyes are open.

GOV.UK says not to wear glasses in your photo unless you have to. If you must wear them, they cannot be sunglasses or tinted, and your eyes must not be covered by the frames or any glare, reflection or shadow.

### Smiling, open mouth or tilted head

**Problem:** You are smiling, your mouth is open, or your head leans to one side. **Cause:** Natural habit when someone takes your picture. **Fix:** Keep your mouth closed and your face relaxed, and look straight into the camera with your head level.

Children under 6 do not have to keep a plain expression or look straight at the camera. See our guide to [UK baby passport photo rules](/how-to/baby-passport-photo-rules-uk) for the full child rules.

## Rejection reasons about the background

### Patterned, dark or creased backgrounds

**Problem:** You can see wallpaper, a door, furniture, a creased sheet or a dark wall. **Cause:** Taking the photo wherever you happen to be. **Fix:** Use a plain light wall with nothing on it.

For printed photos, GOV.UK asks for a plain cream or light grey background. For digital photos, it asks for a plain light-coloured background.

### Edited or replaced backgrounds

**Problem:** The background looks cut out, too even or slightly blurred around your hair. **Cause:** An app replaced or erased the real background. **Fix:** Retake the photo against a real plain wall.

### Background too similar to hair or clothing

**Problem:** Your hair or shoulders blend into the wall. **Cause:** Very light hair against a cream wall, or a light top. **Fix:** Choose the light grey option, or wear a darker top. GOV.UK asks for clear contrast between you and the background.

## Rejection reasons about the file or print

### Low resolution, blurry or pixelated photos

**Problem:** The photo looks soft, grainy or blocky. **Cause:** Using the front camera, digital zoom or a heavily compressed image. **Fix:** Use your phone's rear camera, hold it steady and do not zoom in.

A digital photo must be at least 600 × 750 pixels and between 50KB and 10MB. Screenshots and photos sent through messaging apps are often shrunk too much.

### Wrong size, cropped, or resized incorrectly

**Problem:** Your head is too big, too small or cut off. **Cause:** Cropping by eye, or stretching a photo to fit. **Fix:** Use a tool that crops to the right ratio and head size.

A printed photo must be 35 × 45 mm, with your head 29–34 mm from chin to crown. It must not be a cut-down version of a larger picture. For a digital photo taken on your own device, include your head, shoulders and upper body, and do not crop it, because the online service does that for you. Our guide to [UK passport photo size](/how-to/passport-photo-size-uk) has a quick diagram.

### Wrong paper, glossy prints or damaged prints

**Problem:** The print is creased, marked, faded or on ordinary paper. **Cause:** Home printing on the wrong paper, or careless handling. **Fix:** Print to a professional standard on plain white photographic paper with no border.

Keep prints flat and unmarked on both sides, unless one has to be countersigned. Our guide on how to [print passport photos at home](/print-passport-photos-at-home) explains how to get a clean result.

## Rejection reasons about editing and filters

### Beauty filters and phone auto-enhancement

**Problem:** Your skin looks smooth, your eyes look bigger or your face looks slightly different. **Cause:** Beauty mode, portrait mode or an automatic filter. **Fix:** Turn off all filters and effects before you take the photo.
### Over-brightened or colour-shifted photos

**Problem:** Your skin looks washed out, orange or grey. **Cause:** Editing the brightness or colour, or warm indoor bulbs. **Fix:** Use natural daylight and leave the colours as they are. If you need to brighten a photo a lot, retake it in better light instead.

### Photos older than one month

**Problem:** You used an older photo, or one from a previous application. **Cause:** Reusing a photo you already had. **Fix:** Take a new one.

GOV.UK says your photo must have been taken in the last month. You need a new photo for each new passport, even if your appearance has not changed.

## What to do after a rejection

### Take a new photo step by step

1. **Read the reason you were given.** Look at the message or letter from HM Passport Office, or the warning in the online service, and note exactly what was wrong.
2. **Set up a plain, light background.** Stand in front of a plain light-coloured wall with nothing behind you. For a printed photo, the background must be cream or light grey.
3. **Face a window for soft, even light.** Use daylight from in front of you. Turn off the flash and step about half a metre away from the wall so no shadow falls behind your head.
4. **Get someone to take the photo.** Ask a friend to hold the camera at your eye level. Look straight at the lens with your mouth closed, eyes open and a plain expression.
5. **Switch off filters and editing.** Turn off beauty mode, portrait mode and any auto-enhance setting. Do not brighten, smooth or change the background afterwards.
6. **Check the photo before you use it.** Run the new photo through a passport photo checker, fix anything it flags, then upload it or print it at 35 × 45 mm.

### Will my application be delayed?

Yes, it can be. Sending a suitable new photo quickly is the best way to keep the delay short. If you have a trip booked soon, check the current processing times and urgent options on GOV.UK.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I get my fee back if my photo is rejected?

GOV.UK does not describe a refund for a photo problem. A rejected photo usually means your application is paused while HM Passport Office waits for a suitable photo, rather than cancelled. Follow the instructions in the message or letter you receive, and check GOV.UK or contact HM Passport Office if you are unsure.

### How long does a new photo take to be accepted?

GOV.UK does not publish a set time for checking a replacement photo. It only says your application will be delayed if your photos do not meet the rules. Send a new photo as soon as you can, and check your online account or letters for updates on your application.

### Can I appeal a rejected photo?

GOV.UK does not describe an appeal process for photos. If HM Passport Office needs a new photo, it will contact you and explain what to do. The quickest route is usually to take a new photo that fixes the problem. Contact HM Passport Office if you think there has been a mistake.

### Can I use the same photo for a retry?

No, not if the same problem is still in it. An examiner who rejected the photo is likely to see the same issue again. Take a new photo that fixes the reason you were given. It also needs to have been taken in the last month, so an older photo may not be allowed.

### Does a booth photo get rejected less often?

GOV.UK says photos from a booth or shop are more likely to be approved than a photo taken on your own device. A booth controls the light and background for you. Choose the option to get a photo code if you are applying online, then enter the code during your application.
