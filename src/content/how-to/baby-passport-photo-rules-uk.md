# UK Baby Passport Photo Rules (Size, Eyes Closed and Tips)

> **Quick answer:** A UK baby passport photo follows the same size and quality rules as an adult's: 45 mm high × 35 mm wide when printed, or at least 600 × 750 pixels online. Babies **under 1** can have their eyes closed. Children **under 6** do not have to look at the camera or keep a plain expression. Your baby must be **on their own**, with no toys, dummies or visible hands. Under 1, lay your baby on a plain light-coloured sheet and take the photo from above. The photo must be taken in the last month.

## UK baby passport photo size and requirements

### Size and format table

| :---------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |

| File size         | Not applicable                                                 | 50KB to 10MB                                         |

\*HM Passport Office guidance says the photo-checking template is waived for babies under 12 months, so exact head size is less strict for infants. Aim for a close-up of the full head and upper shoulders.

### Background and lighting

The background must be plain, with no pattern, toys or furniture showing. For prints it must be cream or light grey. For a digital photo it must be plain and light-coloured, with clear contrast between your baby and the background.

There must be no shadows on your baby's face or behind them. Soft daylight from a window works better than a flash or ceiling light.

### What is different from adult photos?

Babies and young children get three relaxed rules:

- Children **under 1** do not have to have their eyes open.
- Children **under 6** do not have to look directly at the camera or have a plain expression.
- Children **over 1 and under 6** do not need a closed mouth, according to HM Passport Office guidance for photographers.

Everything else stays the same. The photo must be clear, in focus, in colour and unaltered by software. Your child must be on their own, with nothing covering their face.

## Rules by age

### Newborns and babies under 1

Your baby's eyes can be open or closed. They do not need to look at the camera or have a plain expression. HM Passport Office also says infants under 12 months do not need a closed mouth, and the photo only needs to be a good likeness.

GOV.UK says babies under 1 should lie on a plain light-coloured sheet, with the photo taken from above. You can support their head with your hand, but your hand must not be visible in the photo. A sleeping newborn is fine, as long as the whole face is visible and nothing else is in the picture.

### Toddlers aged 1 to 5

From the first birthday, eyes should be open. Until the sixth birthday your child does not have to look straight at the camera or keep a plain expression, and HM Passport Office does not need a closed mouth. A small smile or a glance to one side is not a problem.

Toddlers can usually sit up, so you can sit them in front of a plain light wall. They must still be on their own in the photo, with no toys and no dummy.

### Children aged 6 and over

From the sixth birthday, the adult standard applies. Your child must face forwards, look straight at the camera, have a plain expression with their mouth closed, and have their eyes open and visible. Hair must not be in front of their eyes.

Practise the "passport face" a couple of times before the real photo. Glasses are not allowed unless your child has to wear them, and then they must not be tinted and must not cover the eyes or cause glare.

## How to take a baby passport photo at home

GOV.UK lets you take a digital photo on your own device during the application, but you need someone to help. It also says photos from a booth or shop are more likely to be approved than photos taken on your own device. If you keep struggling, a booth or shop is a good plan B.

### For a newborn (lying down, photo from above)

1. **Lay a plain sheet on a flat surface.** Spread a plain, light-coloured sheet with no pattern or creases over a bed or a mat on the floor. For printed photos, a cream or light grey sheet is best.
2. **Lay your baby on their back.** Place your baby in the middle of the sheet. Make sure nothing else is on the sheet, including toys, blankets or a dummy.
3. **Light the room evenly.** Use daylight from a nearby window and turn off the flash, so no shadows fall on your baby's face or the sheet.
4. **Take the photo from directly above.** Hold your phone flat above your baby's face, with the camera pointing straight down. Do not let your own shadow fall across them.
5. **Take lots of shots.** Take several photos in a row. Choose one where your baby's face is straight on, in focus and clear of hands, toys and hair.
6. **Check and size the photo.** Use a baby passport photo tool to check the photo and size it, then upload it or print it at 35 × 45 mm.

Never leave your baby alone on a raised surface. If you use a bed, keep one hand close by, out of shot.

### For a baby who can sit up (tip, not an official rule)

GOV.UK only asks for a plain light background, so you have some freedom. Many parents sit the baby in a car seat or high chair with a plain light sheet draped behind them. Check that the sheet is smooth and that no straps, buckles or padding show near the face.

Hold the camera at your baby's eye level, about one metre away. Ask a second adult to stand behind you to get your baby's attention.

### For a toddler (tip, not an official rule)

Sit your toddler on a chair in front of a plain light wall, about half a metre away so no shadow falls behind their head. One adult takes the photo at eye level while another stands behind the photographer with a toy. The toy must stay out of the picture.

Take a burst of photos and pick the one with open eyes and a clear face.

### Best time of day and how to keep them calm

Pick a time when your baby is fed, rested and happy, often just after a nap. Daylight from a window is best, so morning or early afternoon works well. Set up the room first and take photos in quick bursts.

## What is not allowed in a baby photo

### Dummies and toys

GOV.UK says babies must not be holding toys or using dummies. Remove them before you take the photo, even if your baby is happier with one.

Clear the sheet or chair of soft toys, muslins and blankets too. There must be no other objects in the picture.

### Hands and arms supporting the baby

Children must be on their own in the picture. That means no other people, including a parent's hands, arms or lap.

For babies under 1 you can support their head, but your hand must not be visible. Supporting from behind or under the sheet, out of the frame, works best.

### Hats, bows and hair accessories

A head covering is only allowed for religious or medical reasons, so take off hats and hoods. Nothing may cover your child's face, and there must be no hair in front of their eyes.

Keep the look plain and simple. Large bows or accessories that cast shadows on the face are best left out.

## Printing, uploading and submitting the photo

### Online application (digital photo)

Most parents apply online. You can get a digital photo with a code from a photo booth or shop before you start, and enter the code in your application. Or you can take the photo on your own device during the application, with someone helping you.

The photo must be at least 600 × 750 pixels and between 50KB and 10MB. If you use your own device, include your child's head, shoulders and upper body, and do not crop it. The service crops it for you.

### Paper application (two prints)

For a paper form you need 2 identical printed photos, each 45 mm high × 35 mm wide, in colour, on plain white photographic paper with no border. They must not have creases or tears and must not be a cut-down version of a larger picture.

When you post them, keep the photos separate from each other and loose, not attached to the form. Our guide to [printing passport photos at home](/print-passport-photos-at-home) explains how to get the size right, though a photo lab is safer.

### Confirming your child's identity

How this works depends on how you apply:

- **Online:** after you submit, someone confirms your child's identity online (for a first child passport, and for a renewal if your child is under 12). They get an email from HM Passport Office. They do not sign a printed photo.
- **Paper form:** a countersignatory signs the form and writes on the back of one photo: "I certify that this is a true likeness of", followed by your child's title and full name, then signs and dates it.

### Who can countersign a child's photo?

GOV.UK says a countersignatory is needed on paper for a first child passport, a replacement of a lost, stolen or damaged passport, a renewal for a child aged 11 or under, and a renewal if your child can no longer be recognised from their old photo. They must:

- have known the adult who signed the form for at least 2 years
- be a person of good standing or work in (or be retired from) a recognised profession
- not be related to you by birth or marriage, in a relationship with you, or live at your address
- if you apply in the UK, live in the UK and hold a current British or Irish passport

## Applying for a child passport: what to know

### Fees and validity

A child passport is for children under 16 and is valid for **5 years**. At the time of writing it costs **£66.50 online** or **£80 on a paper form** from the Post Office. Fees change, so check GOV.UK before you apply.

### Who applies and what you need

Someone with parental responsibility applies. For a first passport you usually need your child's birth or adoption certificate. Check the current list of documents on GOV.UK before you start.

### Post Office Digital Check and Send

Some Post Office branches offer a Digital Check and Send service where staff help with a child's application. At the time of writing the Post Office lists a **£16** fee on top of the passport fee. Check with your branch for current details and availability.

## Common reasons a baby passport photo is rejected

Most baby photo problems come from light, background or editing. For a full list, see why [passport photos get rejected](/how-to/passport-photo-rejected-uk).

### Shadows and background problems

Shadows from your arms, your phone or a cot edge are common in photos taken from above. Hold the phone directly over your baby and switch off lamps and flash.

Creased sheets, patterned bedding and visible pillows can also cause problems. Smooth the sheet flat and fill the frame with plain background.

### Closed eyes on a child over 1

The eyes-closed allowance only applies to babies under 1. After the first birthday, eyes should be open and visible. If your child blinks a lot, turn off the flash and take a long burst of photos.

### Blurry, edited or old photos

Babies move, so blur is common. Use good daylight and the rear camera, and do not zoom in.

Do not smooth skin, remove marks or replace the background with an app. GOV.UK says photos must be unaltered by computer software. Mirror images are also not accepted, and the photo must be taken in the last month.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can my baby's eyes be closed in a UK passport photo?

Yes, if your baby is under 1. GOV.UK says children under 1 do not have to have their eyes open. This means a sleeping newborn is fine, as long as the whole face is visible. From the first birthday, eyes should be open. Taking many shots helps you catch a good one.

### Can I hold my baby in the passport photo?

Your baby must be on their own in the photo, so you cannot appear in it. For a baby under 1, you can support their head with your hand, but your hand must not be visible. Laying your baby on a plain light sheet and taking the photo from above is usually easiest.

### Does my baby have to look at the camera?

No, not if your child is under 6. GOV.UK says children under 6 do not have to look directly at the camera or have a plain expression. From age 6, your child must face forwards, look straight at the camera and keep a plain expression with their mouth closed.

### Can my baby have a dummy in the photo?

No. GOV.UK says babies must not be holding toys or using dummies in their passport photo. Remove the dummy just before you take the picture and take a quick burst of shots. Feeding or settling your baby first can keep them calm without it.

### Can my baby's mouth be open?

For babies under 12 months and children over 1 and under 6, HM Passport Office guidance says a closed mouth is not required. From age 6, the mouth must be closed. The photo must still be a clear, good likeness of your child.

### What background colour does a baby passport photo need?

For a printed photo, the background must be plain cream or light grey. For a digital photo, it must be plain and light-coloured, with clear contrast between your baby and the background. A plain light sheet works well for a baby lying down, as long as it has no creases.

### How old can my baby's passport photo be?

The photo must have been taken in the last month. Babies change quickly, so the photo needs to show how your child looks now. You also need a new photo for every new passport, even if you think your child's appearance has not changed.

### Can I take my baby's passport photo at home?

Yes. For an online application you can take the photo on your own device during the application, with someone helping you. GOV.UK says booth or shop photos are more likely to be approved, so use one if you keep having problems.

### Does a baby passport photo need to be countersigned?

It depends on how you apply. Online, someone confirms your child's identity by email and nobody signs a printed photo. On a paper form, a countersignatory signs the form and the back of one photo for a first child passport and some other applications.
