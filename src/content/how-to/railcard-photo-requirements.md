# Railcard Photo Requirements (16-25, 26-30 and Other Railcards)

> **Quick answer:** Most railcards need a passport-style photo. You can take a selfie or ask someone to take it. Use a plain, light background, show your head and shoulders, and let your face fill about 70% of the frame. Upload a clear, unedited colour photo as a JPG or PNG of 5MB or less.

## What photo do I need for a railcard?

Railcard says it follows "the same rules as the DVLA and Passport Agency". In plain terms, the photo must clearly show your face and look like you today.

### Digital photo requirements

For a digital railcard, you upload a photo during your application. The official guidelines say:

- The photo must be of you, and you must be instantly recognisable
- Portrait style, with your head and shoulders visible
- A plain, light background
- Clear, in focus and in full colour
- No digital edits or filters
- No other objects or people
- No sunglasses or clothing that hides your face

Your face should fill about 70% of the frame, much like a passport photo. Hats and hair must not cover your face.

### Paper application photo requirements

If you buy a railcard at a station, you'll usually be asked for a passport-style photo. A standard UK passport-size print is the easiest option.

Railcard doesn't publish a separate print size. A passport photo at 35 mm wide × 45 mm high, like the ones from a booth, fits the passport-style rule.

### Size and file format

| Requirement | Railcard rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| File types | JPG, JPEG or PNG (some pages also list BMP and GIF) |
| Maximum file size | 5MB |
| Framing | Head and shoulders, face about 70% of the frame |
| Background | Plain and light |
| Colour | Full colour preferred |
| Editing | No edits or filters |
| Who takes it | You (selfie) or someone else |

The 16-25 Railcard FAQ says colour is preferred but black and white is acceptable. Colour is still the safer choice.

## 16-25 and 26-30 Railcard photo rules

These are the two most popular photo railcards. Both use the same general photo guidelines.

### 16-25 Railcard

You need a passport-style photo for the 16-25 Railcard, whether you buy it online or at a station. Online buyers upload a digital photo as part of the application.

Mature students also need a passport-style photo, along with their signed mature student form and student ID.

### 26-30 Railcard

The 26-30 Railcard is digital only, so you'll upload your photo online. You also need ID to prove your age.

Because there's no physical card, the photo appears in the Railcard app. Ticket inspectors compare it with your face, so it must clearly look like you.

### Differences between the two

The photo rules are the same for both cards. The main difference is that the 16-25 Railcard can be bought at a station, while the 26-30 card is digital only.

That means a printed photo is only relevant for some 16-25 applications. For the 26-30 card, you only need a good digital file.

## Other railcards (Senior, Two Together, Family and Friends, Disabled Persons)

Not every railcard asks for a photo in the same way. Check the card you're buying before you start.

### Which railcards need a photo

| Railcard | Photo needed? |
| :--- | :--- |
| 16-25 | Yes, online and at a station |
| 26-30 | Yes (digital only) |
| Senior | Yes, if you choose a digital railcard |
| Two Together | Yes, one photo of each named holder |
| Disabled Persons | Yes, plus proof of disability |
| Family & Friends | Check at checkout (see below) |

The Family & Friends Railcard site gives mixed wording. One answer says each named holder needs a photo for a digital card, while another doesn't limit this to digital. Check the instructions on screen when you apply.

### Who must be in the photo on joint cards

The Two Together Railcard asks for a passport-style photo of yourself and the other named card holder. Each person needs their own separate photo.

Never put two people in one photo. The guidelines say no other people should appear in the picture.

## How to take a railcard photo at home

You don't need a booth. A phone, a plain wall and daylight are enough.

### Step-by-step

1. **Stand in front of a plain, light wall.** Choose a wall with no pattern, pictures or objects. Stand a short step away from it so you don't cast a shadow.
2. **Face the light.** Face a window in daylight so your face is evenly lit. Turn off the flash.
3. **Frame your head and shoulders.** Hold the phone at eye level, or ask someone to take it. Your face should fill about 70% of the frame.
4. **Look straight at the camera.** Keep your face clear of hair, hats and sunglasses. A plain expression is the safest choice.
5. **Take a few shots without filters.** Switch off filters and beauty mode. Take several photos and pick the sharpest one.
6. **Check the file before uploading.** Make sure it's a JPG or PNG of 5MB or less, then check it looks like you and is in focus.

### Background and lighting

A plain white, cream or light grey wall works well. Avoid doors, wallpaper, shelves and anything that adds pattern.

Daylight from a window in front of you is best. Light from above or behind causes shadows that hide your features.

### Face position and expression

Keep your head straight and look directly at the lens. Your face and the top of your shoulders should be visible.

Railcard doesn't publish an expression rule. Because it follows passport-style rules, a plain expression with your mouth closed is the safest option.

### Checking your photo before you upload

Zoom in on the photo. Look for blur, shadows, glare on glasses and hair over your face.

## How to upload your railcard photo

You upload the photo during your online application or in your Railcard account.

### On a phone

Take the photo first, then choose it from your gallery when asked. Check that your phone saves photos as JPG, not a format like HEIC.

If your iPhone saves HEIC files, change the camera format to "Most Compatible" in Settings, or use our [railcard photo tool](/tool/photo-railcard) to export a JPG.

### On a computer

Move the photo from your phone to your computer, then upload it from your files. Check the file is 5MB or less.

If the file is too big, crop it closer to your head and shoulders or resize it before uploading.

### What to do if the upload fails

Railcard suggests these steps:

1. Check the file size and format.
2. Sign out and sign back in.
3. Restart your browser.
4. Clear your browser cache.
5. Use the latest version of your browser.
6. Make sure JavaScript is switched on.

If it still fails, contact [Railcard customer support](https://www.railcard.co.uk/help/contact).

## Why railcard photos get rejected

Railcard says your photo may be rejected if it doesn't meet its guidelines. It will ask you for a more suitable image.

### Common mistakes

- A busy, dark or patterned background
- Sunglasses, hats or hair covering your face
- Your face too small in the frame
- Filters, beauty mode or other edits
- Other people or objects in the photo
- A blurry or dark photo
- An old photo that no longer looks like you

### How to fix and resubmit

Take a new photo against a plain, light wall, facing a window. Fill about 70% of the frame with your face and switch off all filters.

Then upload the new photo when Railcard asks for one. For more on common mistakes, see [why passport photos get rejected](/how-to/passport-photo-rejected-uk).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a selfie for my railcard?

Yes. Railcard says you can take a selfie or have someone take the photo for you. Hold the phone at eye level, face a window and keep your head and shoulders in the frame. Switch off filters and beauty mode, as edited photos break the guidelines and may be rejected.

### Can I use my passport photo for a railcard?

Usually, yes. Railcard follows the same rules as the DVLA and Passport Office, so a recent passport photo fits the passport-style rule. If you have a digital copy, upload it as a JPG or PNG under 5MB. Make sure it still looks like you today.

### Do I need a new photo when I renew?

Railcard doesn't publish a clear rule about this. When you renew, follow the instructions on screen. If your appearance has changed a lot, use a new photo, because the photo must look like you and ticket inspectors compare it with your face.

### Can I smile in a railcard photo?

Railcard doesn't publish a specific rule on smiling. Because it follows DVLA and passport-style rules, a plain expression with your mouth closed is the safest choice. A gentle, natural look is fine, but avoid a wide smile that changes the shape of your face.

### Can I wear glasses in a railcard photo?

Railcard only bans sunglasses and anything that hides your face. Clear glasses aren't specifically banned, but glare on the lenses can hide your eyes. If you wear glasses, tilt them slightly or face away from bright reflections. Taking them off is the safest option.
