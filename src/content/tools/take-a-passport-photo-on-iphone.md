## How to Take Passport Photo With iPhone in 6 Steps

Follow these six steps in order. Each one removes a common cause of rejection.

### Step 1: Set Up Your Space

Good light and a clean background make the biggest difference.

- **Face a window.** Let daylight fall evenly on your face, with no shadows.
- **Avoid direct sun.** Harsh light creates shadows and makes you squint.
- **Stand in front of a plain wall.** Keep frames, doors and furniture out of the shot.
- **Stand about one metre from the wall.** This reduces shadows behind your head.
- **Wear a plain top.** Choose a colour that contrasts with the wall.

![Home setup for an iPhone passport photo: window, plain wall and helper](https://res.cloudinary.com/dipzpwbbk/image/upload/v1790741177/iPhone-passport-photo_xhp0et.webp)

### Step 2: Adjust Your iPhone Camera Settings

A few quick changes give you a cleaner, more usable picture.

| Setting    | What to do                                                                   |
| :--------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Camera     | Use the rear camera, not the selfie camera                                   |
| Mode       | Choose **Photo**, not Portrait, to avoid artificial background blur          |
| Flash      | Turn it off                                                                  |
| Live Photo | Turn it off                                                                  |
| Filters    | Set to **Original** and avoid beauty effects                                 |
| Grid       | Turn on **Grid** in Settings > Camera to help you centre your head           |
| Format     | Choose **Most Compatible** in Settings > Camera > Formats to save JPEG files |

The Most Compatible setting matters because iPhones save HEIC files by default. Some official portals only accept JPEG.

### Step 3: Position Your iPhone

Camera height and distance control how natural your face looks.

- **Hold the phone at eye level.** A camera that points up or down distorts your face.
- **Stand about one to 1.5 metres away.** A closer camera stretches your nose and forehead.
- **Keep the phone upright.** Shoot in portrait orientation.
- **Leave space around your head.** The tool needs room to crop correctly.

If your iPhone has a 2x option, use it. It reduces distortion compared with shooting close on the 1x lens.

### Step 4: Pose and Take the Photo

Now take the shot.

- **Face the camera.** Look straight at the lens with your head level and your shoulders square.
- **Keep a neutral expression.** Close your mouth and keep both eyes open.
- **Show your full face.** Move hair away from your eyes and eyebrows.
- **Remove your glasses.** Many countries no longer accept them, except for documented medical reasons.
- **Use the timer.** Open the Camera app, tap the timer icon and choose 3 or 10 seconds.
- **Take several shots.** Choose the sharpest, most natural picture.

Ask your helper to press a volume button as the shutter. This avoids camera shake.

### Step 5: Check Your Photo

Open the picture in the Photos app and zoom in.

- Is your face sharp, with clear eyes?
- Is the light even, with no shadow on your face or the wall?
- Is your head straight, centred and fully in the frame?

Retake the photo if you answer no to any question. A retake takes one minute.

## How to Take a 2x2 Passport Photo on iPhone

Need the US format? A 2x2 photo is a square that measures 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm). You can take the photo the same way, then square it up in your iPhone.

1. Open the photo in the **Photos** app and tap **Edit**.
2. Tap the **Crop** icon.
3. Choose **Square** in the aspect ratio options.
4. Keep your head in the middle, with space above it.
5. Upload the result to our tool and select the US passport or visa option.

Our tool then sets the head height, background and file size. US digital uploads must be square, between 600×600 and 1200×1200 pixels, and no larger than 240 KB.

## How to Print Your iPhone Passport Photo

Paper applications need printed photos. You have two easy options.

1. **Print at a shop or kiosk.** Save the 6 × 4 inch (10 × 15 cm) print sheet to your iPhone, take it to a pharmacy or photo kiosk, and ask for a 6 × 4 inch print with no cropping.
2. **Print at home with AirPrint.** Open the sheet, tap **Share**, tap **Print** and choose your printer. Load glossy or matt photo paper and select the highest quality.

Then measure one photo with a ruler to confirm the exact size. Cut along the guides with a trimmer or sharp scissors. Some applications also ask for a countersignature on the back of one photo, so read your form.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you take a passport photo on iPhone?

Yes. An iPhone camera is sharp enough for a passport photo. Take a clear, front-facing picture against a plain wall, then use a tool to size it and check the background. Follow your country's rules.

### How do I take a passport photo with my iPhone by myself?

Set your iPhone on a stand or a stack of books at eye level, about one metre away. Open the Camera app, turn on the timer, step into position and take several shots. A helper gives you better results, but the timer works.

### What iPhone settings should I use for passport photos?

Turn off flash, Live Photo and filters. Turn on the grid to centre your head. Choose Most Compatible in Settings > Camera > Formats so your iPhone saves JPEG files instead of HEIC.

### Can I make a 2x2 photo on iPhone?

Yes. Open the photo in the Photos app, tap Edit, tap Crop and choose Square. Then upload it to our tool, which sets the head size, background and file size for US requirements.

### Can I print a passport photo from my iPhone?

Yes. Use AirPrint or take the print sheet to a pharmacy or photo kiosk. Print at 100% scale on photo paper and measure one photo to check the size.

### Will my iPhone passport photo definitely be accepted?

No one can promise that. The issuing authority makes the final decision, but careful setup and our checks help you avoid the most common mistakes.
