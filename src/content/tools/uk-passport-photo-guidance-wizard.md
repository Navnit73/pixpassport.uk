### 1.1 Step 1: Upload or Take Your Photo

After you read your guidance, choose a photo from your camera roll or take a new one. GOV.UK lets you take a digital photo on your own device during your application, but you need someone to help you. Ask a friend or family member to hold the camera instead of taking a selfie.

GOV.UK also notes that photos from a booth or shop are an alternative option. If you use a booth or shop, choose the option to get a photo code, which you can enter directly during your online application.

### 1.2 Step 2: Separate Requirements for Digital vs Paper Applications

Digital and printed passport applications have fundamentally different formatting rules. Understanding which format you need ensures your submission goes smoothly:

- **Digital Application (Online on GOV.UK):** GOV.UK requires an uncropped digital image showing your head, shoulders, and upper body. You must not crop it closely yourself because the GOV.UK online system crops the photo automatically during submission. Your digital file must be at least 600 × 750 pixels, between 50KB and 10MB, in colour, and unaltered by software filters.
- **Paper Application (Printed Form):** Paper submissions require 2 identical printed photographs measuring exactly **35 mm wide by 45 mm high**. The distance from the crown of your head to your chin must be between 29 mm and 34 mm. They must be printed on plain white photographic paper with no borders.

## 2. Guidance for Every Applicant Type

The rules below follow the published GOV.UK guidance. If you searched for **passport photo standards UK** applicants must meet, or for a **biometric passport photo UK** officials will accept, this is the information you need. ### 2.1 Adults and Older Children

Adults and children aged 6 and over follow the same pose rules. In your photo you must:

- face forwards and look straight at the camera
- have a plain expression and your mouth closed
- have your eyes open and visible
- have no shadows on your face or behind you
- have nothing covering your face

The photo must also contain no other objects or people, and it must not have "red eye". The background must be in clear contrast to you, so avoid a light top against a light wall.

#### 2.1.1 Glasses, hair and jewellery

- **Glasses:** GOV.UK says not to wear glasses unless you have to. If you must wear them, they cannot be sunglasses or tinted glasses, and your eyes must not be covered by the frames or by any glare, reflection or shadow.
- **Hair:** Keep hair away so none falls in front of your eyes.
- **Jewellery:** GOV.UK does not list jewellery on its photo pages. The rule that covers it is that nothing may cover your face and there must be no shadows, so remove anything that does either.

#### 2.1.2 Head coverings for religious reasons

GOV.UK says you must not wear a head covering in your photo unless it is for religious or medical reasons. Your face must still be fully visible, with nothing covering it and no shadows across it. If you need extra support with your photos, GOV.UK offers help with passport photos if you are disabled.

### 2.2 Babies and Special Circumstances

Different rules apply to young children, and the wizard shows the right ones for the age you enter.

- **All children:** The child must be on their own in the picture.
- **Babies:** They must not hold toys or use dummies.
- **Children under 6:** They do not have to look directly at the camera or have a plain expression.
- **Children under one:** They do not have to have their eyes open. You can support their head with your hand, but your hand must not be visible in the photo.
- **Lying down:** Children under one should lie on a plain light-coloured sheet, and you should take the photo from above.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where can I find official UK passport photo guidance?

The official guidance is on GOV.UK, on the "Get a passport photo" pages. They cover digital photos and printed photos, with separate rules for babies and children. We base this wizard on those pages, but we are an independent service and not affiliated with GOV.UK or HM Passport Office.

### What are UK biometric photo standards?

People often search for a **biometric photo UK** passport applicants can use, meaning a photo that meets the passport rules. GOV.UK sets out those rules as quality, size and appearance requirements. Your photo must be clear, in focus, in colour and unaltered, with a plain light background, a forward-facing pose, a plain expression with your mouth closed, and your eyes open and visible. A digital photo must be at least 600 × 750 pixels and between 50KB and 10MB.

### Do the standards differ for children?

Yes. The size and background rules stay the same, but the pose rules relax for young children. Children under 6 do not need to look at the camera or keep a plain expression, and children under one do not need to have their eyes open. A child must always be on their own in the picture, and babies must not hold toys or use dummies.

### Can I take my digital passport photo on a phone?

Yes. You can take a clear, high-resolution photo on your smartphone or digital camera with a friend helping you (no selfies). Ensure even lighting, look straight ahead with a neutral expression, and our tool will verify framing and compliance before you submit.

### Do I need to print my digital passport photo?

No, if you are applying online through GOV.UK, you only need the digital photo file. If you are completing a physical paper form, you need 2 identical 35 × 45 mm prints on white photographic paper.
