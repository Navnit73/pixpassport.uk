# ICAO Photo Standards: Requirements and Free Online Photo Check

**ICAO photo standards** are the international rules for the face photo in a biometric passport or travel document. They are published by the International Civil Aviation Organization in **Doc 9303** and are based on the ISO/IEC 19794-5 face image standard. They cover how your face is positioned, lit and framed so that machines and border officers can match you to your photo.

**Quick answer:** an ICAO compliant photo shows your whole face looking straight at the camera, with a neutral expression, eyes open, even lighting, no shadows or glare, a plain light background, and enough resolution for at least **90 pixels between the centres of your eyes**. The exact photo size in mm or pixels is set by the country that issues your document, not by ICAO.

## What Are ICAO Photo Standards?

### ICAO in Plain English

ICAO (the International Civil Aviation Organization) is a United Nations agency. Its **Doc 9303** sets the global specifications for machine readable travel documents, including passports. Part of that document explains what a passport face image must look like, so a photo taken in one country can be read by systems in another.

### ICAO Photo Standard vs ISO/IEC 19794-5

Doc 9303 refers to **ISO/IEC 19794-5**, the technical standard for face image data. ISO defines the measurable face image properties, and ICAO adopts them for passports. When people search for the "ICAO biometric photo standard", they usually mean these two documents working together.

### ICAO vs Country Passport Photo Size

ICAO does not set one universal print size. Each issuing country decides the photo size, background colour and file rules, while keeping within the ICAO face image principles. That is why a UK, US and Australian photo can all be ICAO based yet be different sizes.

#### Why this matters if your photo is rejected

A photo can follow ICAO face rules but still be rejected because it is the wrong size, colour or file type for your country. Always check both.

##### ICAO sometimes typed as "ICO"

Some people search for an "ICO compliant photo". It is the same thing: they mean ICAO.

## ICAO Passport Photo Size: mm, Pixels and Resolution

### ICAO Photo Size

ICAO does not give a single mm size. Common examples set by countries include:

| Country or use | Photo size |
| :--- | :--- |
| **UK printed passport photo** | 35 × 45 mm |
| **US visa and passport photo** | 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), square |
| **Australian passport photo** | 35–40 × 45–50 mm |
| **Finland digital photo** | 500 × 653 px |

### ICAO Biometric Photo Size in Pixels

The key ICAO resolution rule is a minimum of **90 pixels between the centres of the eyes**. A passport-size photo scanned at 300 dpi gives about this much detail, so low-resolution phone crops and heavily enlarged photos are a common reason for failing.

### ICAO Photo Format and File Size

Format and file size depend on the country and the application system. JPEG is the most common format. Chip images are stored compressed and are small, so avoid over-compressing the face, which loses the detail biometric systems need.

## ICAO Photo Specifications in Detail

### Face and Head Position

#### Centred and straight

Your face must be in the centre of the frame, looking directly at the camera with your head upright.

#### Full head visible

The whole head must be visible, from the top of the hair to the bottom of the chin, with the shoulders and a little space around the head.

### Expression and Eyes

#### Neutral expression

Keep a neutral expression with your mouth closed. Smiling changes the shape of your face and can reduce biometric match accuracy.

#### Eyes open and visible

Both eyes must be open and clearly visible, with no hair, frames or reflections covering them. Avoid red-eye.

### Lighting and Shadows

Light must be even across the face. There should be no shadows on the face or on the background behind you, and no flash reflections on skin or glasses.

### Background

Use a plain, uniform, light background with no patterns, objects or other people. Many countries name a specific colour, such as light grey, cream or white.

### Glasses, Head Coverings and Accessories

#### Glasses

Many countries now ask you to remove glasses. If they are allowed, there must be no glare and the frames must not cover your eyes.

#### Head coverings

Allowed only for religious or medical reasons in most countries, and your full face from chin to forehead must remain visible.

#### Jewellery and piercings

These must not cover the face or cause reflections.

### Image Quality

The photo must be sharp, correctly exposed, in natural colour and free of filters or retouching. Beauty modes and heavy smoothing can cause a rejection.

## How to Take an ICAO Compliant Photo at Home

1. **Ask someone to take it.** Selfies distort the face and rarely give straight, even results.
2. **Use a plain, light background,** and stand about half a metre in front of it to avoid shadows.
3. **Put the camera at eye level about 1.5 metres away** and zoom in slightly rather than standing close.
4. **Light your face evenly from the front,** with the light behind the camera.
5. **Look straight at the camera** with a neutral expression, mouth closed and both eyes open.
6. **Check the photo,** then crop and resize it to your country's required size.

## Frequently Asked Questions About ICAO Photo Standards

### What are ICAO photo standards?

ICAO photo standards are the international rules in ICAO Doc 9303 for the face photo in a biometric passport. They cover head position, expression, lighting, background and image resolution, so machines and border officers can match you to your photo.

### What is an ICAO compliant photo?

An ICAO compliant photo shows your whole face looking straight at the camera, with a neutral expression, eyes open, even lighting, no shadows or glare, a plain light background, and at least 90 pixels between your eye centres.

### What is the ICAO passport photo size?

ICAO does not set one photo size. Each country sets its own, for example 35 × 45 mm in the UK and 2 × 2 inches in the US.

### What is the ICAO photo size in pixels?

There is no single pixel size. The key ICAO resolution rule is at least 90 pixels between the centres of the eyes. Your country may also set a pixel size for digital uploads.

### What is the ICAO photo format?

ICAO bases its face image rules on ISO/IEC 19794-5. The file format depends on the country and the application system, with JPEG being the most common.

### Is ICAO the same as ISO/IEC 19794-5?

They are related but not the same. ISO/IEC 19794-5 defines face image data, and ICAO Doc 9303 refers to it for passport photos.

### What is the ICAO biometric photo standard?

It is the set of face image rules in ICAO Doc 9303, based on ISO/IEC 19794-5, that make a photo suitable for automated face recognition in passports and border control.

### Can I smile in an ICAO photo?

No. ICAO requires a neutral expression with your mouth closed, because smiling changes your facial features and can reduce biometric match accuracy.

### Can I wear glasses in an ICAO photo?

It depends on your country. Many now ask you to remove glasses. If they are allowed, there must be no glare or tint, and the frames must not cover your eyes.

### What background does an ICAO photo need?

A plain, uniform, light background with no patterns, objects or shadows. The exact colour is set by your country, for example cream or light grey in the UK.

It means your photo failed a face or image-quality rule, such as head tilt, shadows, a smile, glare, a patterned background or low resolution. Fix the specific problem and re-check the photo, and also confirm that the size matches your country's rules.
