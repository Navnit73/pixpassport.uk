**ICAO photo standards** are the international rules for the face photo in a biometric passport, visa or ID card. They are published by the International Civil Aviation Organization in **Doc 9303** and are based on the ISO/IEC 19794-5 face image standard.

**Quick answer:** an ICAO compliant photo shows your whole face looking straight at the camera, neutral expression, eyes open, even lighting, no shadows or glare, a plain light background, and at least **90 pixels between the centres of your eyes**. The exact photo size is set by the country that issues your document, not by ICAO.

## ICAO Photo Standards: Requirements at a Glance

| Requirement            | ICAO standard                                          |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Face position**      | Full face, centred, looking straight at the camera     |
| **Head pose**          | Head upright, not tilted, turned or lowered            |
| **Expression**         | Neutral, mouth closed, eyes open and clearly visible   |
| **Eye resolution**     | At least 90 pixels between the centres of the eyes     |
| **Lighting**           | Even across the face, no shadows, no red-eye, no glare |
| **Background**         | Plain, uniform and light, no pattern or objects        |
| **Image quality**      | Sharp, natural skin tone, colour, not retouched        |
| **Glasses**            | No glare, no tint, frames must not cover the eyes      |
| **Size and file type** | Set by the issuing country                             |

### Face and Head Position

Your face must be centred and looking directly at the camera with your head upright. The whole head must be visible, from the top of the hair to the bottom of the chin, with the shoulders and a little space around the head. Many countries ask for the face to fill roughly 70–80% of the photo height (about 32–36 mm on a 35 × 45 mm photo), but the exact figure is set by your country.

### Expression and Eyes

Keep a neutral expression with your mouth closed. Smiling changes the shape of your face and can reduce biometric match accuracy. Both eyes must be open and clearly visible, with no hair, frames or reflections covering them.

### Lighting and Shadows

Light must be even across the face. There should be no shadows on the face or on the background behind you, and no flash reflections on skin or glasses.

### Background

Use a plain, uniform, light background with no patterns, objects or other people. Many countries name a specific colour such as white, off-white, cream or light grey. Avoid clothing that blends into the background.

### Glasses, Head Coverings and Jewellery

- **Glasses:** many countries now ask you to remove them. If allowed, there must be no glare and the frames must not cover your eyes.
- **Head coverings:** allowed only for religious or medical reasons in most countries, with the face visible from chin to forehead.
- **Jewellery and piercings:** must not cover the face or cause reflections.

### Image Quality

The photo must be sharp, correctly exposed, in natural colour and free of filters or retouching. Beauty modes and heavy smoothing can cause a rejection.

## ICAO Photo Size, Pixels and Resolution

### ICAO Passport Photo Size

ICAO does not set one universal print size. Each issuing country decides the photo size, background colour and file rules while keeping within the ICAO face image principles.

| **US visa and passport photo** | 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), square |
| **Australian passport photo**  | 35–40 × 45–50 mm                  |

### ICAO Photo Size in Pixels

The key ICAO resolution rule is a minimum of **90 pixels between the centres of the eyes**. For a 35 × 45 mm photo, 300 dpi gives about 1050 × 1350 pixels, which is comfortably above that minimum. A small phone crop or a heavily enlarged image can fall below it, which is a common reason for failing.

### ICAO Photo Format and File Size

Format and file size depend on the country and the application system. JPEG is the most common format. Avoid over-compression, which removes the facial detail biometric systems need.

## ICAO Photo Standard vs Country Photo Rules

### ICAO vs ISO/IEC 19794-5

ISO/IEC 19794-5 defines the measurable face image properties, and ICAO Doc 9303 adopts them for passports. When people search for the "ICAO biometric photo standard", they usually mean these two documents together.

### Which Documents and Countries Use ICAO Photo Standards?

ICAO photo rules apply to passports, visas, identity cards and other government ID, and are followed by most countries, including the UK, the US, Australia, New Zealand and EU member states. Each country adds its own size, background and file rules on top, so a UK, US and Australian photo can all be ICAO based yet differ in size.

### Why a Photo Can Pass ICAO Rules but Still Be Rejected

A photo can follow the ICAO face rules and still be rejected because it is the wrong size, colour or file type for your country. Always check both.

### ICAO or "ICO"?

Some people search for an "ICO compliant photo". It is a common misspelling: they mean ICAO.

## "Photo Does Not Comply With ICAO Standards": Common Errors and Fixes

If an application says your photo is **not valid as per ICAO standards** or shows an **ICAO compliance issue**, one of these is almost always the cause:

| Problem ❌                        | Fix ✅                                                             |
| :-------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Head tilted or turned             | Face the camera with your head level and upright                   |
| Smiling or mouth open             | Neutral expression, mouth closed                                   |
| Shadows on the face or background | Stand away from the wall and light your face evenly from the front |
| Patterned or dark background      | Use a plain, uniform light background                              |

| Eyes not clearly visible          | Pin hair back and open both eyes fully                             |

| Blurred or over-compressed        | Use a sharp photo and avoid heavy compression                      |
| Filters or retouching             | Use an unedited photo                                              |

## How to Take an ICAO Compliant Photo at Home

1. **Ask someone to take it.** Selfies distort the face and rarely give straight, even results.
2. **Use a plain, light background** and stand about half a metre in front of it to avoid shadows.
3. **Put the camera at eye level about 1.5 metres away** and zoom in slightly rather than standing close.
4. **Light your face evenly from the front,** with the light behind the camera.
5. **Look straight at the camera** with a neutral expression, mouth closed and both eyes open.
6. **Take several shots,** pick the sharpest, then crop and resize it to your country's size.
